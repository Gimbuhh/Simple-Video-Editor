## 1.1.0 — 2026-11-03

### Breaking Changes

- Projects saved by 1.1 use a new format that older versions cannot read. Keep your original project files and the 1.0.4 app available for rollback.

### Added

- Timeline filmstrips show sampled frames, and a live clip preview shows where dragged recordings will land.
- HDR recordings support native HDR preview and HDR10 export, or convert to SDR with stronger contrast, color and shadow detail. Tiny bright cursors do not dim the rest of the scene during conversion.
- Audio can be separated for independent trimming and movement, with per-item volume, mute, fades and track selection. Matching audio and video can be linked again. Audio extending past the video plays against a black background.
- Framing controls fit, fill, crop or rotate individual sections while leaving recordings untouched.
- HEVC (H.265) is available alongside H.264 and AV1 export.

### Changed

- The timeline fits automatically when you add or duplicate sections, and the normal mouse pointer stays over clip bodies.
- Hardware decoding keeps supported recordings responsive in preview. Scrubbing updates the video as you drag and selects the exact frame on release. Clicking the timeline keeps playback running; edge clicks and frame navigation select the actual retained frames, including variable-frame-rate game captures.
- A cleaner graphite workspace puts the video first, with clearer text, larger recording previews, blue trim handles and a new scissors icon. Export stays at the top right, export settings are in the sidebar, and quick trims are close by in clip menus.
- Preview automatically reconnects to your Windows audio output when it becomes available again.
- The Rust rewrite removes the bundled .NET runtime and reduces the measured portable download by about 25% compared with 1.0.4. Memory use depends on the editing workflow.
