## Breaking Changes

- Projects saved by 1.1 use a new format that older versions cannot read. Keep your original project files and the 1.0.4 app available for rollback.

## Added

- Timeline filmstrips show sampled video frames, and dragged recordings keep their thumbnail visible.
- HDR footage can stay in HDR through preview and export, or convert to SDR when needed.
- Audio controls let you choose a track, change volume, mute or fade each section.
- Framing controls fit, fill, crop or rotate individual sections while leaving recordings untouched.
- HEVC (H.265) is available alongside H.264 and AV1 export.

## Changed

- Scrubbing keeps the preview visible and updating while you drag, then settles on the exact frame when you release.
- Preview seeks faster on ordinary SDR recordings and uses hardware decoding when supported.
- The editor has a graphite design with clearer text, visually centered button labels, larger recording previews and blue trim handles. Export stays at the top right; extra settings stay out of the way. Clip edges show a horizontal resize cursor for trimming.
- Preview uses your Windows audio output and automatically retries if it becomes unavailable.
- The app has a new scissors icon with a blue play symbol.
- The editor runs on Rust with a smaller portable package. The compared candidate's download is about 25% smaller than 1.0.4 and omits the bundled .NET runtime.
- Frame navigation and quick trims use the actual frames in variable-frame-rate recordings, including game captures.

## Known limitations

- This is an unreleased candidate. Some workflows use more memory than 1.0.4; the [C#/Rust comparison](../docs/rust-csharp-comparison.md) explains the measured benefits and tradeoffs.
- Sections use hard cuts. Transition effects are planned.
- Mixed-DPI transitions, manual HDR toggles and full screen-reader workflows still need final acceptance. See the [rewrite status](../docs/rust-rewrite-status.md) for the remaining checks.
